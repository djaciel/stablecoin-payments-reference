# Stablecoin Payments Reference

The runnable companion to an article on stablecoin payments. It shows what sits around a
stablecoin payment: a double-entry ledger, explicit payment states, a durable workflow, deposits
matched to partners, reconciliation against the chain, and failures you can cause on purpose. It is not a product, not
a library and not an ERC20 tutorial. It runs on a public testnet (Base Sepolia) with test USDC only.

## What you need

Docker with Compose v2. That is all you need to run the demo: no Node, no keys to paste, no
environment variables. (Node is for contributors, see [the last section](#for-contributors).)

## Start it

In the folder you cloned:

```sh
docker compose up
```

This starts seven services: Postgres 16, Temporal and its web UI, the api, the worker, the signer
and the ui. The first start builds one image (it installs the dependencies inside it) and
downloads Postgres and Temporal, so it takes a few minutes. Later starts reuse all of that.

On the first start the stack writes what a fresh clone does not have:

- `.keys/company/company.seed`: the company seed, generated by the signer.
- `.keys/partner/`: a second seed that plays the partners' wallets (demo scaffolding).
- the database schema, and two partners, **Northwind Logistics** and **Bluefin Studio**, each
  with a deposit address of its own.

Both seeds stay on your disk (`.keys/` is gitignored), are never printed, and must never hold
real money. The signer is the only process that reads the company seed: every company address
comes from it by index (index 0 is the treasury, index *n* is the deposit address of partner *n*),
and the signer publishes no port, so only the other containers can ask it anything. A real
treasury key would live in custody infrastructure, not in a file next to the code.

Then open:

- `http://localhost:5173/admin`: the company's side.
- `http://localhost:5173/partner`: what a partner sees.

The browser only talks to `localhost:5173`. The ui forwards everything under `/api` to the api
container, so there is no CORS setting, API URL or environment variable to get right.

To check the api from a terminal:

```sh
curl -s http://localhost:3000/health
# {"status":"ok","database":"up"}

curl -s http://localhost:3000/treasury
# the treasury address, its balances on chain and in the books, as JSON
```

## Fund the treasury

`/admin` opens on **Start**. It shows the **Treasury** (its address and its onchain balance), what
the **Books** say (Available, Reserved, Partner debt), and a **Fund the treasury** panel. The panel stays until the treasury holds both USDC and ETH on chain.

The treasury needs **both**, from two different faucets:

- **Testnet USDC**, the money the demo moves: the [Circle faucet](https://faucet.circle.com).
  Pick Base Sepolia and paste the treasury address.
- **Base Sepolia ETH**, the network's own currency, which pays the gas: the
  [Coinbase Developer Platform faucet](https://portal.cdp.coinbase.com/products/faucet) (it asks
  you to sign in). Fund partner wallet stays disabled until the treasury holds at least 0.0051 ETH,
  because that button passes 0.005 ETH on to the partner wallet.

With USDC and no ETH, nothing can be sent. A payout costs well under 0.000001 ETH in gas (measured
on this repo's own test payouts), so one drop of ETH goes a long way. Other gas faucets, their
limits, and what to do when one refuses you: [`docs/dev/faucets.md`](docs/dev/faucets.md).

**What you should see.** The treasury's onchain balance shows the new figures on its next read. Books stay at zero for a
little longer: once the funding's block is final (that takes minutes on Base Sepolia), the
watcher posts it within a tick or two, and Available shows the same USDC figure as the chain. That
gap closing is the first thing the demo teaches: money on chain is not money in the books until
something posts it. The gas never enters the books.

Until the money is in, three things do not work yet:

- **Pay** is refused at the door: a FAILED payment is recorded (`insufficient_funds`) and nothing
  is reserved.
- **Fund partner wallet** is disabled until the treasury holds 2 USDC and 0.0051 ETH on chain,
  and the USDC in the books.
- `/partner` has nothing to pay with, because the partner wallet is funded from the treasury.

## Your first payout

The examples below assume the faucet gave the treasury 20 USDC. The figures are the ones the
Books show, with six decimals.

1. On `/partner`, press **Fund partner wallet**, then **Send**. It sends 2 USDC and 0.005 ETH from
   the treasury to the wallet the partners pay you from, which your first deposit needs. It is not
   a payment, so the USDC leaves the books as `demo:partner-funding` and the gas is not posted.
2. On **Payables**, under **New invoice**, pick Northwind Logistics, type `0.10` and a description,
   and press **Create**.
3. On the invoice's card, press **Pay**, then **Pay** again in the dialog. The card now shows
   **Payment** (its state) and **Reference** (`PAY-` and four digits).
4. Click the reference. The payment's story shows each step as it happens: **Created**,
   **Reserved**, **Signed**, **Sent**, **Paid**, **Settled**. The payment's state moves
   `CREATED → RESERVED → SENT → SETTLED` (the other two ends, FAILED and REVIEW, are what
   [Break it](#break-it) shows). The wait between Sent and Paid is the network making the block
   final, which takes minutes.
5. On the story, **The transfer on Base Sepolia** opens the real transaction in the explorer, and
   **Its runs in Temporal** opens the workflow that ran the steps (`http://localhost:8080`).
   Temporal keeps finished runs for 30 days; the payment's story stays in the database for good.

What the Books say along the way:

| Moment | Available | Reserved | Partner debt |
|---|---|---|---|
| After Fund partner wallet | 18.000000 | 0.000000 | 0.000000 |
| After Create | 18.000000 | 0.000000 | 0.100000 |
| After Pay | 17.900000 | 0.100000 | 0.100000 |
| After Settled | 17.900000 | 0.000000 | 0.000000 |

## Your first deposit

1. On **Receivables**, under **New invoice**, pick Northwind Logistics, keep `0.05`, type a
   description and press **Create**. Partner receivables goes up by 0.05.
2. Open `/partner`. Under **You are**, pick Northwind Logistics. In **What you owe**, press
   **Pay** on the invoice, then **Pay** in the dialog. The invoice turns PAYING.
3. On **Transfers**, under **Money that arrived, newest first**, the deposit shows as DETECTED
   within seconds: seen, not credited. Once its block is final it turns SETTLED and the invoice
   turns PAID. On **Receivables**, Deposits goes up by 0.05 and Partner receivables goes down by
   0.05.

The company knows this money is Northwind's because it arrived at the deposit address only
Northwind was given, not because of the amount or the sender.

Two tabs this path does not use: **Partners** shows every partner's two addresses (and adds
partners), and **Pay to an address** pays an address on the allowlist, now or at a time you pick.

## It checks itself

On **Reconciliation**, press **Run now**. A new run appears at the top, headed **Books and chain
agree**, with three lines:

- **Books**: Available + Reserved + Deposits.
- **Chain at block N**: the treasury plus each deposit address, read at that one fixed block.
- **Explained by what was in flight**: payments the chain has already moved and the books have
  not settled yet, or the other way round.

Below it, one line per payment and per deposit says what explains it. If the heading reads
**Something is not explained**, the books and the chain differ by money nothing in flight accounts
for, and that is what a person has to look at. The check also runs by itself every ten minutes:
in the Temporal UI (`http://localhost:8080`) three workflows keep running, `watcher`,
`dispatcher` and `reconciler`.

## Break it

**Break it**, the last tab, has five cards. **Run it** on a card does the whole experiment and
leaves the evidence on the card. Each one writes and pays its own 0.02 USDC invoice, except the
race, and most of them take a few minutes because they wait for a final block. Run them one at a time.

- **Double click**: the same invoice is paid twice at the same instant, with the same key, and
  one payment comes out of it.
- **The race**: 1,500 available and two payments of 1,000 at once; one is reserved, the other is
  refused. It runs in a sandbox database of its own, so it spends nothing and takes seconds.
- **Crash after signing**: the worker dies between signing and sending, and the payment finishes
  itself with the bytes it had already signed.
- **Node not responding**: the node stops answering, and the payment goes to REVIEW with its
  money still reserved until you resume it.
- **Revert the next payment**: the transfer reaches the chain and reverts. No stablecoin moves,
  the reserved money goes back to Available once the revert is final, and it costs only gas.

Four of them also run from a terminal, through the same api (they need Node 22 and
`pnpm install`, see below):

```sh
pnpm experiment:double-click
pnpm experiment:race
pnpm experiment:crash
pnpm experiment:stuck      # the node not responding
```

To stop the worker by hand while a payment waits, see
[`docs/dev/restart-survival.md`](docs/dev/restart-survival.md).

## Ports, configuration, teardown

Nothing has to be set. The stack publishes five ports, on `127.0.0.1` only: 5173 (ui), 3000
(api), 8080 (Temporal UI), 7233 (Temporal) and 5432 (Postgres). If one is taken on your machine,
copy `.env.example` to `.env` and change `UI_PORT`, `API_PORT`, `TEMPORAL_UI_PORT`,
`TEMPORAL_PORT` or `POSTGRES_PORT`. Two host commands do not read `.env`: the `pnpm experiment:*`
commands call `http://localhost:3000` and `pnpm test:db` calls Postgres on 5432, so with other
ports set `API_URL` or `POSTGRES_PORT` in your shell for them.

- `docker compose down` removes the containers and keeps the database. The next
  `docker compose up` comes back as you left it.
- `docker compose down -v` also deletes the database: the books, the invoices, the payments and
  the audit log. The seeds stay on your disk, so the treasury keeps its address and its money. The
  new watcher only looks about 5.5 hours back (10,000 blocks), so older funding is not posted
  again: the books start at zero next to a chain balance that is not, and a reconciliation run
  reports a mismatch, correctly. New funding is posted as usual. To keep your books, keep the
  volume.
- Deleting `.keys/` starts over with a new, unfunded treasury. If you replace the company seed,
  use `down -v` as well: the database holds nonces and addresses of the old treasury, and a payout
  from the new one would never land.

## For contributors

The checks and the experiment commands run on your machine, so they need Node 22 (not 24: a
native module Temporal needs does not install there) and pnpm (`corepack enable` gives you the
version this repo pins).

```sh
pnpm install
pnpm typecheck   # tsc over the whole workspace
pnpm lint        # eslint, type-aware
pnpm test        # the pure tests; no Docker
pnpm test:db     # against the stack's Postgres, in a payments_test database of its own
```

`pnpm test` covers the seed and the key derivation, the rule that decides a transfer happened, the
migration runner, and that the repo still uses the article's states, account names, reference
format and figures. `pnpm test:db` covers the ledger's rules and the payment's state changes
against a real Postgres, concurrent payments included, so a rule someone breaks fails there first.
